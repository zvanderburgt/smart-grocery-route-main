# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary users are meal planners and families managing recurring weekly grocery trips. They use the app in practical, often time-pressured contexts: planning from home, adding items while multitasking, and checking off items in-store while moving through aisles.

Their core job is to build a reliable list quickly, then complete shopping with minimal backtracking and fewer forgotten items.

## Product Purpose

Breadcrumb exists to reduce planning and shopping friction by combining fast list capture (typing, voice, OCR, barcode, recipe import) with adaptive route intelligence.

Success means users spend less time in-store, forget fewer items, and trust that the list order increasingly matches how they actually shop over repeated trips.

## Positioning

The only grocery list that learns your exact in-store route trip after trip.

## Brand Personality

Calm, dependable, and quietly smart. The interface should feel supportive and efficient rather than flashy, with clear feedback that builds trust in the learning behavior over time.

## Anti-references

Avoid a playful gamified shopping experience, ad-heavy coupon-app aesthetics, and enterprise dashboard visual language. The product should never feel noisy, manipulative, or over-instrumented.

## Design Principles

1. Learn from behavior, not setup: The app should improve from real check-off order without burdening users with manual configuration.
2. Keep capture friction near zero: Any item source should enter the list quickly with minimal correction effort.
3. Optimize for one-handed, in-motion use: Controls, spacing, and feedback should work reliably in real shopping environments.
4. Make intelligence visible but unobtrusive: Show confidence and suggestions clearly without interrupting the primary task.
5. Build trust through consistency: States, labels, and interactions should remain predictable across all input and shopping flows.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal accessibility target is set yet. The product should still prioritize legible text contrast, clear interaction states, large touch targets, and reduced-friction interactions suitable for varied in-store conditions and device usage contexts.
